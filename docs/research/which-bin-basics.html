Which bin? The Brisbane basics (red, yellow, green)

Updated 2026-08-30 · sources checked at build time

<p>Brisbane-area households juggle up to three wheelie bins — and the rules genuinely change when you cross a council border. 1 in 4 Australians report finding the recycling system confusing [1].</p> <h3>The three bins (typical SEQ setup)</h3> <ul><li><strong>General waste (red or dark lid):</strong> landfill-bound. Soft plastics, nappies,</li></ul> <p>polystyrene, broken ceramics — the stuff no processor wants.</p> <ul><li><strong>Recycling (yellow lid):</strong> containers and paper/cardboard. Empty, loose</li></ul> <p>(not bagged), and clean-ish. Bagged recycling is treated as contamination.</p> <ul><li><strong>Garden organics (green lid):</strong> lawn clippings, branches, leaves. Some SEQ</li></ul> <p>councils are moving to <strong>FOGO</strong> (food organics + garden organics), which adds food scraps — check your council before adding food to green.</p> <h3>The classic confusion items</h3> <div style="overflow-x:auto"><table><thead><tr> <th>Item</th> <th>Usual answer</th> <th>Why</th> </tr></thead><tbody> <tr><td>Pizza box</td><td>Recycle if clean; grease-soaked parts to general waste</td><td>Grease contaminates paper fibre</td></tr> <tr><td>Soft plastics (bags, wrap)</td><td>General waste in most areas</td><td>Not accepted kerbside; some supermarkets take them</td></tr> <tr><td>Batteries</td><td>Never kerbside — fire risk</td><td>Drop-off points at libraries/tip shops</td></tr> <tr><td>Nappies</td><td>General waste, bagged</td><td>No processor accepts them kerbside</td></tr> <tr><td>Clothing/textiles</td><td>Donation bins first; worn-out to general waste</td><td>Kerbside recycling can't take textiles</td></tr> <tr><td>E-waste (TVs, laptops)</td><td>Never kerbside</td><td>Council drop-off events / transfer stations</td></tr> </tbody></table></div> <p>Residents also report overflow pressure: one Sydney household's "unrecyclable" weekly load didn't even fit one red bin [2], and contaminated recycling gets whole loads dumped when too much is mixed in [3]. The fix is boring but real: sort at the bin, keep recycling loose and unbagged, and check your own council's A-Z before guessing.</p> <h3>Source register</h3> <ol><li><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/aug/15/bin-sins-australia-recycling-confusion">Guardian Australia — "bin sins": 1 in 4 find recycling confusing (15 Aug 2026)</a></li><li><a href="https://x.com/roodave">X — @roodave, 27 Aug 2026</a></li><li><a href="https://x.com/aussiewongm">X — @aussiewongm, 26 Aug 2026</a></li></ol>
