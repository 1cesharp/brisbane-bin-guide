Which bin? The Brisbane basics (red, yellow, green)

Updated 2026-08-31 · sources checked at build time

Brisbane-area households juggle up to three wheelie bins — and the rules genuinely change when you cross a council border. 1 in 4 Australians report finding the recycling system confusing [1].

The three bins (typical SEQ setup)

  • General waste (red or dark lid): landfill-bound. Soft plastics, nappies,

polystyrene, broken ceramics — the stuff no processor wants.

  • Recycling (yellow lid): containers and paper/cardboard. Empty, loose

(not bagged), and clean-ish. Bagged recycling is treated as contamination.

  • Garden organics (green lid): lawn clippings, branches, leaves. Some SEQ

councils are moving to FOGO (food organics + garden organics), which adds food scraps — check your council before adding food to green.

The classic confusion items

Item Usual answer Why
Pizza boxRecycle if clean; grease-soaked parts to general wasteGrease contaminates paper fibre
Soft plastics (bags, wrap)General waste in most areasNot accepted kerbside; some supermarkets take them
BatteriesNever kerbside — fire riskDrop-off points at libraries/tip shops
NappiesGeneral waste, baggedNo processor accepts them kerbside
Clothing/textilesDonation bins first; worn-out to general wasteKerbside recycling can't take textiles
E-waste (TVs, laptops)Never kerbsideCouncil drop-off events / transfer stations

Residents also report overflow pressure: one Sydney household's "unrecyclable" weekly load didn't even fit one red bin [2], and contaminated recycling gets whole loads dumped when too much is mixed in [3]. The fix is boring but real: sort at the bin, keep recycling loose and unbagged, and check your own council's A-Z before guessing.
