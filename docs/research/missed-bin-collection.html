Missed bin collection — what to do in Brisbane

Updated 2026-08-30 · sources checked at build time

<p>A missed bin collection is one of the most common bin complaints in Brisbane — and reports have been rising: "missed bin reports have been exploding in Brisbane each year since 2022. Basics aren't being done" [1].</p> <h3>What to do, in order</h3> <ol><li><strong>Check the calendar first.</strong> Public holidays shift collection days by one</li></ol> <p>(or to the next service day). Confirm your day on your council's bin-day page.</p> <ol><li><strong>Check placement.</strong> Bin out by the night before, wheels to the kerb, 30cm+</li></ol> <p>gap from cars/other bins, lid closed, not overfilled. Overfull or blocked bins can legally be skipped.</p> <ol><li><strong>Report it to your council the same day.</strong> All SEQ councils take missed-bin</li></ol> <p>reports online or by phone. The cutoff matters: report within ~24-48h of collection day and crews usually come back within a day or two; later reports can mean waiting until your next scheduled collection.</p> <ol><li><strong>If it keeps happening</strong>, log each report — councils use repeat reports to</li></ol> <p>fix route problems with their contractors.</p> <h3>Why it spikes</h3> <p>Contractor shortages, route changes after FOGO rollouts, and post-holiday volume all drive missed collections [1] [2].</p> <h3>Source register</h3> <ol><li><a href="https://x.com/paulytaylor_d">X — @paulytaylor_d, 26 Aug 2026</a></li><li><a href="https://au.news.yahoo.com">Yahoo News — Rockingham FOGO bin confusion (30 Jun 2026)</a></li></ol>
