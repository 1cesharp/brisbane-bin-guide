Skip bin size calculator — what size skip do I need?

This calculator converts what you're actually throwing out — wheelbarrow loads, trailer loads and bulky items — into cubic metres, then recommends a bin size with sensible headroom. It covers the common Australian sizes: 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10 m³.

How this is worked out

  • Wheelbarrow = 0.07 m³ heaped; 6×4 box trailer = 0.8 m³ sensibly loaded.
  • Bulky items use typical loose volumes: couch 2.5 m³, mattress 0.5 m³, fridge 0.7 m³.
  • We add ~20% headroom, because skips should never be filled above the rim and loose junk always stacks worse than you expect.
  • Estimates are for general household/mixed waste. Volumes vary — when you're between sizes, go up.

What size skip do I need? — FAQ

What size skip do I need for a kitchen renovation?
A small kitchen strip-out (cabinets, benchtop, old appliances) typically fills 4–6 m³. Add tile or rubble and weight becomes the limit, not volume.
How much fits in a 2 m³ skip?
Roughly 8–10 wheelbarrow loads, or a couple of trailer loads — suits a small garden clean-up or one room of decluttering.
Should I size up or down if I'm between sizes?
Size up. Overfilled skips can't be legally transported and drivers will refuse collection or ask you to offload.
Can I put soil or concrete in a general skip?
Usually no, or only in limited quantities. Heavy waste needs a purpose-rated bin — check our council and provider pages before booking.
